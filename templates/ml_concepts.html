MODEL TRANSPARENCY

Model & limitations

A plain-language description of what the model does, how it works, and what its output does not mean.

01

What kind of model?

MindPulse uses a Random Forest classifier. It combines the votes of 180 decision trees, each limited to a maximum depth of 10, to assign the answers to one of three project categories: Low Risk, Moderate Risk, or High Risk.

02

How a result is produced

Your answers → input preparation → 180 decision trees → combined category estimate → model-factor display and suggestions. Numeric missing values are filled with training-data medians, missing categories with the most common category, and physical activity is one-hot encoded.

03

Inputs it uses

Age; sleep duration (hours); sleep quality; stress; mood; physical activity (Low/Moderate/High); exercise sessions per week; daily screen time (hours); work/study hours; social interaction; anxiety; and work/study satisfaction.

The form accepts age 17–100, 1–10 ratings, 3–12 sleep hours, 0–7 exercise sessions per week, and 1–14 hours for screen time and work/study. These are self-reports. The current form does not explain every scale’s anchors consistently; the direction implied by a rating is an assumption, not a clinical standard. Camera images and face-analysis results are not inputs to this prediction model.

04

Training data and labels

The project dataset is synthetic and educational. Its Low/Moderate/High labels are generated by applying a weighted score to selected lifestyle inputs and dividing that score into roughly thirds. This teaches the model patterns created by the project—not verified rules about real people.

The generated training examples cover ages 17–64, while the form accepts ages up to 100. Age and physical activity are model inputs, but they are not directly included in the synthetic label-scoring formula.

05

How it was evaluated

The code holds out 20% of the dataset for testing, with class proportions kept similar, and reports accuracy, weighted precision, weighted recall, and weighted F1.

These scores measure performance on a held-out portion of synthetic data only. They do not establish real-world, student-specific, or clinical accuracy.

06

Probabilities and explanations

The model also returns class probabilities. They are not validated or calibrated as confidence scores, so they should not be read as the chance that a person truly has a wellbeing condition.

Displayed factor importance combines global feature importance with simple direction rules. It is not a causal explanation of an individual result.

07

Important limitations

This is an educational reflection tool, not a diagnosis, screening standard, treatment recommendation, or emergency assessment. Estimates can be wrong and may not fit someone’s circumstances. Do not use them for medical decisions or to decide who deserves support.

Support options are available to everyone, regardless of their result. View India-wide support options →

Face analysis is separate and experimental. It uses simple image heuristics to describe visible expression; it does not measure mental health and does not feed into this model.
